OverEasy

Applications: Figma, Illustrator, Photoshop

OverEasy is a mobile app concept that reimagines the cooking experience by helping users stay organized and reduce food waste.

Brief

The goal was to design a tool that not only simplifies cooking but also promotes a decluttered and efficient home.

The app introduces a feature that encourages users to use ingredients that would typically go unused, helping them better understand and manage what they already have in their kitchens.

Cooking has become deeply embedded in modern culture, especially with the rise of food content across social media platforms.

This shift has made traditional recipe apps feel stagnant and less adaptable to the needs of today’s users. With so much free and engaging content readily available online, conventional cooking apps often struggle to show any value.

OverEasy is designed to give an experience that goes beyond just simple recipes. Its goal is to engage users through a personalized and dynamic perspective.

Process

Ideation. Many premockups were drawn up to integrate the necessary components without overwhelming the user with unnecessary clicks or tasks.

Since the app introduces new apps or tabs that are unique, it was important to convey that message without forcing the user to read a manual instruction simply to use my app.

Flow and Wireframe. In order to create a seamless flow throughout the app, it was imperative to the users journey to identify the differences between the key tabs and the secondary tabs. Creating a clear hierarchy let me give spotlight to the features that made this cooking app different from competitors.

Prototype. The first created prototype had a multitude of problems.

  • Color: The main colors used were simply too light, so it was hard to showcase the hierarchy between sections, words, and buttons.
  • User Flow: When implementing certain points of the wireframe to a prototype, it became apparent that it took too many clicks to use certain actions ruining a users journey.
  • Pain Points: Some implemented features were very tedious to use, or took too many steps in many case scenarios.

The solutions were:

  • Color: New colors with many different shades were newly implemented to create a balance on the app.
  • User Flow: New directions were added in many different places to ensure that the user could reach the tabs they needed to be in faster, and more efficiently.
  • Pain Points: Simple buttons and actions were added to ensure that some scenarios could be done faster and easier.

Research

To better understand user behaviors and pain points, I conducted interviews with 8 participants across various age groups. Each participant was asked whether they had used a cooking or recipe app within the past year, and if not, why.

The majority of participants reported not using any dedicated cooking apps, due to the lack of need. Most indicated that they find recipes easily through search engines or social media making separate apps unnecessary.

  • 8 Individuals

Persona. Joanna comes back from a hard day of work. She is exhausted and wants a simple meal she can make with the ingredients she already has. She then opens up the app and sees all the available recipes she can make with the current ingredients she has. She then decides to make a simple meal that only takes her 20 minutes to finish.
