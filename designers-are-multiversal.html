Designers are Multiversal

Applications: Illustrator, Claude, HTML, SVG, JavaScript

A generative data visualization of the SCAD Graphic Design curriculum, where 35 courses become a field of stars and every kind of design work draws its own constellation across them.

Brief

The brief was to turn real data into a poster. I chose the program I study in. On paper a curriculum reads like a ladder, one course after another. In practice it works nothing like that. A course that looks niche, like Visual Analysis of Static Content, turns up inside a poster, a dashboard, an exhibit and a brand.

So the poster makes one argument. Every course is a building block, and the same blocks recombine into very different kinds of work. Adaptability is not an option for a designer, it is the requirement.

Design

The Field of Stars. The curriculum is set as eight rings around five core principles, with GRDS 201 Introduction to Graphic Design at the center. Every ring is made of the course descriptions themselves, set in full. The text is held at 45% white so it reads as a quiet wireframe beneath the lines.

The Constellations. Nine categories of design work are drawn across that field as colored routes. Visual communication and posters, spatial and experiential design, experience and interaction, motion and time based media, information and data, packaging, digital interface, editorial and publication, and brand identity. Change the category and the same stars make a different picture. Draw again and the same category finds another route.

Motion Poster. The motion poster stacks the nine categories one by one. Each arrives through quick cuts between alternate versions, then settles on top of the others until all nine stand together. Then the field clears and a new set of routes begins. The loop is 15 seconds and never repeats the same routes.

The motion poster plays on load at the top of this page.

Static Posters. Ten static compositions, one with all nine categories together and one for each category on its own.

The Interactive Poster. The poster also lives on the web. Visitors can zoom into every ring to read the courses, switch between categories, change the line weight and draw new routes. The recording below shows the system generating variations.

Process

Setting the Rules. Before anything was drawn, I wrote down the rules the poster had to follow. GRDS 201 sits at the center, and GRDS 205, 285, 301, 323 and 348 surround it as the five core principles, with no first step among them. Every other course belongs to one principle and sits in that principle’s fifth of the circle. Each ring holds one course per principle, in course number order. The groups are uneven on purpose, so the outer rings thin out and the empty slices are real gaps in the data.

Fitting the Type. Type size comes from the longest entry, GRDS 440 Studio III at 754 characters plus its title. On each ring, the type is set at the largest size where that entry still fits its slice, so the type grows ring by ring and every description fits.

Drawing the Routes. A course joins a category when its title or description contains one of that category’s keywords. Each line is a straight, closed route through three or more courses of one category, and routes never pass through 201 or the five principles. No two routes are alike. Each takes its own set of courses, its own order, its own weight from a hairline to a heavy stroke, and its own opacity.

Key Decisions. Prerequisites were set aside, because the piece treats every course as a building block, not a step. Full descriptions stayed in instead of excerpts, so every word is readable on the A1. Undergraduate and graduate courses share one field. Studio and practice first held eleven courses and pulled the circle out of shape, so three courses moved to the principles they fit just as well. The lines are straight, because curvature carries no meaning in the data. The colored lines are the message and the descriptions are the structure, so the lines carry all the color and weight.

Working with AI. I set the concept, structure, sketch and poster layout in Illustrator. Claude was used as a critical partner on the strategy, and then to extract the catalog text, measure every description, fit the type to the rings and write the HTML, SVG and JavaScript. Every rule above was decided before any code was written.

Research

Every word on the rings comes from the SCAD catalog pages for the B.A., B.F.A., M.A. and M.F.A. in Graphic Design and Visual Experience. That is 35 GRDS and GDVX course descriptions, set in full. The left text column on the poster is SCAD’s own description of the program, and the right column states the design strategy.

Some gaps are part of the data. GDVX 779 Graduate Internship has no description in the catalog, so its slice carries its title only. GRDS 201 and the five core principles carry no description by design, because they are the center the rest is built on. The catalog’s standard safety sentence was left out of the keyword matching, so it would not connect courses that share nothing else.

Takeaway

The data did not need decoration, it needed rules. Once the rules were set, the poster could draw itself thousands of ways and still say the same thing. The same courses, read differently, make a different designer every time.
