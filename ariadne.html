Ariadne

RoleGraphic Designer TeamTonisha Taylor, Mckenzie Murray, Tobey Kim CourseMethods of Contextual Research InstructorProfessor Chunshik

Ariadne is an app concept designed to help Gen Z consumers understand the sustainability, quality, and longevity of the clothing they buy, making smarter fashion decisions easier than throwing something away.

The Problem

Fast fashion produces 10% of global pollution, more than international flights and maritime shipping combined. 85% of textiles end up in landfills each year, and 80 billion garments are consumed globally, 400% more than two decades ago. Yet sustainable fashion remains inaccessible: using the Mall of Georgia as a reference point, there is one dedicated sustainable or ethical fashion store for every twenty fast fashion brands.

The core challenge: how can Gen Z easily understand the sustainability, quality, and longevity of the clothing they purchase?

Research

A cultural probe was conducted across three locations, Bob Jones Trail in Avila Beach, CA; Sugar Loaf Mills in Duluth, GA; and Kirkwood Park Trail in Atlanta, GA, reaching 74 participants. The team built a stakeholder map spanning users, developers, and experts, with structured discussion guides tailored to each group.

Experts included a former Nike designer, a UX researcher at TheRealReal, and Mikki Taylor, Professor of Fashion Design and Marketing at Howard University, who noted: "The cycle for clothing used to be on a seasonal basis like every quarter. Now there's new clothing out every week. Where's that stuff going?"

Key finding: when asked what they consider more when buying clothes, 44% of Gen Z respondents said they prioritize quality over low prices, but a significant gap remains between awareness and behavior.

App Solution

Ariadne addresses the problem across five connected features, each mapped to a specific research insight.

Price Per Wear

Purchased clothing often goes unused or is discarded quickly, contributing to both waste and financial loss. Ariadne adds a Price Per Wear tag, calculated as times worn divided by dollars spent, that surfaces the true cost and quality of a garment, empowering shoppers to decide whether an item is worth buying before they commit.

Premium Quality

Fast fashion deprioritizes material quality under pressure from high production volume and micro-trend cycles. A user interview surfaced the issue directly: "I got a top from Target. It got a sweat stain on it, so it never came out, so I had the item for one day." Ariadne's quality feature educates shoppers on natural fabrics, cotton, linen, silk, and identifies synthetic fabrics to avoid, making a durable wardrobe easier to build.

Sustainability Score

Gen Z consumers are environmentally aware, yet still account for a significant portion of fast fashion consumption, partly because many brands use misleading sustainability language. Ariadne's Sustainability Score draws on materials and sourcing data to give each item a clear rating, inspired by approaches like TheRealReal's content badges. The higher the score, the more sustainable the purchase.

Incentivizing Circularity

Rather than discarding clothing, Ariadne guides users through a Clothing Decision Guide: evaluate the item's condition, answer a few questions, and receive a next step, repair, repurpose, donate, resell, or recycle. Items sent in for repair incur a small fee; damaged pieces can be exchanged for shopping credit through resale partners.

Brand Transparency

Greenwashing is widespread, and difficult for the average shopper to identify. As one expert noted: "The H&M Conscious Collection was busted because people thought they were donating clothes to make recycled clothes, but the only thing H&M recycled was the hang tag." Ariadne includes an in-app guide explaining common sustainability terms so consumers can evaluate brand claims before purchasing.

Promotion

Influencer marketing heavily shapes Gen Z purchasing behavior, often toward fast fashion. Ariadne's promotional strategy targets the "frugal chic" niche on YouTube and TikTok, a lifestyle movement combining financial savvy with intentional, quality-focused spending, to reach an audience already primed for the message.

Brand System

The Ariadne identity is built on two colors, a warm orange (#F47D25) and a deep teal (#00577A), conveying energy and trust without veering into the typical green palette associated with sustainability. The typeface is Figtree, a geometric sans-serif that keeps the visual language clear, modern, and approachable for a Gen Z audience.
